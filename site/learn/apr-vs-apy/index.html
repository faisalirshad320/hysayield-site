APR vs APY

One is what you pay, the other is what you earn — and they are not calculated the same way.

APR (annual percentage rate) is the yearly cost of borrowing, used for loans and credit cards. APY (annual percentage yield) is the yearly return on a deposit. APR ignores compounding; APY includes it.

On the same underlying rate, APY is always the higher number. That makes APY flattering on savings and APR flattering on debt — which is worth remembering when you read either.

Side by side

APRAPY
Used forLoans, credit cards, mortgagesSavings, CDs, money market accounts

Why the same rate gives two numbers

A credit card at 24% APR compounding daily costs about 27.1% a year in practice — the APY equivalent. The card issuer quotes 24% because the rules for loans say to. A savings account at 4.40% compounding daily pays 4.50% APY, and the bank quotes 4.50% because the rules for deposits say to. Each rule happens to show the consumer-facing side in its better light.

The practical upshot

  • Compare savings on APY, loans on APR, and never one against the other.
  • To see what debt really costs, convert its APR to an effective annual rate using the converter.

Questions

What is the difference between APR and APY?

APR is the cost of borrowing without compounding; APY is the return on saving with compounding. APR is for loans, APY for deposits.

Is APR or APY higher?

On the same underlying rate, APY is higher, because it includes compounding and APR does not.
