How much to keep in a high-yield savings account

Enough to cover emergencies and near-term plans — and less than you might think beyond that.

Keep your emergency fund — commonly three to six months of essential expenses — plus money for goals in roughly the next few years. Beyond that, long-term money usually belongs in investments.

There is no maximum, but deposits over $250,000 at one bank in one ownership category are not FDIC-insured; split larger sums across banks.

Three layers

  1. Emergency fund. Three months of essentials is a floor; six is common; more if your income is irregular or you are the sole earner. On $3,200 of monthly essentials, six months is $19,200, which earns $768 a year at 4% APY. Work out your own number.
  2. Known expenses within a few years — a car, a house deposit, tuition, a wedding. Money you cannot afford to see fall in value.
  3. Nothing much else. Cash you will not need for many years loses ground to inflation after tax. See HYSA vs investing.

Is there such a thing as too much?

Two limits matter. The first is insurance: $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. Joint accounts and certain trust accounts are separate categories, so a couple can insure more at one bank. The second is opportunity cost: the real after-tax return on savings can be close to zero.

How many high-yield savings accounts should you have?

One is enough for most people. Reasons to have more:

  • Over the insurance limit — a second bank insures the excess.
  • Separate goals — many banks offer "buckets" or sub-accounts inside one account, which does the same job without extra logins.
  • Rate chasing — possible, but the gain on a small balance is often a few dollars a year.

Several accounts at the same bank in the same ownership category share one $250,000 limit.

Questions

How much money should I keep in a HYSA?

Your emergency fund — commonly three to six months of essential expenses — plus money for goals in the next few years.

How many high-yield savings accounts should I have?

One is enough for most people. A second bank makes sense if you are above the $250,000 FDIC limit.

Is it smart to keep a lot of money in a high-yield savings account?

It is safe, but after tax and inflation the real return can be close to zero, so money you will not need for many years usually does better invested.
