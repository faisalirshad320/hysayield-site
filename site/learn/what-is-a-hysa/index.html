What is a high-yield savings account?

An ordinary savings account at a better rate — and the handful of things that make it different.

A high-yield savings account (HYSA) is a savings account paying a much higher rate than a typical branch account — often ten times or more — usually because it is run by an online bank without branch costs. The FDIC national average savings rate is 0.37% (September 21, 2026); that average is the "normal" a HYSA is high relative to.

It is still a bank deposit: FDIC-insured to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. The catch is that the rate is variable and can be cut at any time, and transfers out take a business day or two.

How it works

  1. You open the account online and link it to your checking account.
  2. You move money in by transfer. Interest accrues on the balance, usually daily, and is credited monthly.
  3. You move money out by transfer back to checking, typically arriving in one to three business days.

Why the rate is higher

Online banks carry no branch network, so their costs per dollar deposited are lower, and they compete for deposits on rate. Branch banks rely on convenience and existing relationships instead. The account type is the same; the business model is different.

What a HYSA is good for

  • An emergency fund, where access matters more than squeezing out the last basis point.
  • Short-term goals with no fixed date — a deposit, a car, a trip.
  • Cash waiting to be invested, earning something in the meantime.

What it is not good for

  • Long-term growth. After tax and inflation, a HYSA roughly holds value; it does not grow it much. Set tax and inflation in the calculator to see this for your numbers.
  • Everyday spending. Transfers take days, and some accounts cap withdrawals per month.
  • Locking a rate. For that you need a CD — see CD vs HYSA.

Before you open one

  • Confirm the bank is FDIC-insured, or NCUA-insured for a credit union.
  • Check for a minimum balance, a balance cap on the headline rate, or monthly fees.
  • Check how many withdrawals a month are allowed and how long transfers take.
  • Compare on APY, not on a plain rate — see what APY means.

Questions

How does a high-yield savings account work?

You deposit money by transfer, it earns interest at a variable rate (usually accrued daily and paid monthly), and you withdraw by transfer back to checking, typically within one to three business days.

Are high-yield savings accounts safe?

At an FDIC-insured bank, deposits are covered to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. The rate itself is not guaranteed.

Can you lose money in a high-yield savings account?

Not the deposit itself at an insured bank, within the limits. After inflation and tax, though, the purchasing power can fall.
