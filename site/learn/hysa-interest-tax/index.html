Is high-yield savings interest taxable?

Yes — and it changes what the account actually earns you.

Yes. In the US, interest from a high-yield savings account is taxed as ordinary income in the year it is credited, at your federal marginal rate and usually your state rate too. Your bank issues a Form 1099-INT once you earn $10 or more in a year; the interest is taxable even below that.

On $25,000 at 4.50% APY, a year's interest is $1,125.00. At a 22% federal and 5% state rate, $303.75 goes in tax, leaving $821.25 — an effective 3.29% return.

How it is taxed

  • Ordinary income, not capital gains. It is added to your wages and taxed at your marginal bracket.
  • In the year it is credited, whether or not you withdraw it. Leaving interest in the account does not defer the tax.
  • State tax too, in most states. A few have no income tax.
  • Reported on Form 1099-INT, which banks issue at $10 of interest or more. Below $10 it is still taxable income.

What that does to the return

Tax turns the headline APY into something smaller. The after-tax yield is roughly APY × (1 − your combined rate). At 4.50% APY:

12%3.96%
22%3.51%
27% (22% + 5% state)3.29%
37%2.84%

Take inflation off that as well and the real return can be close to zero. Enter your own brackets and inflation under "Tax and inflation" in the calculator to see it for your numbers.

Worth knowing

  • Treasury bills and I bonds are exempt from state and local income tax, which can make a slightly lower yield better after tax in high-tax states.
  • Tax-advantaged accounts such as an IRA can hold cash too, deferring or removing the tax, with their own rules on access.

This page describes the general US treatment. Your situation may differ; a tax professional can advise on it.

Questions

Do you pay taxes on a high-yield savings account?

Yes. The interest is taxed as ordinary income at your federal and usually state rate, in the year it is credited, whether or not you withdraw it.

Do I get a 1099 for my savings account?

Your bank issues Form 1099-INT if you earn $10 or more of interest in the year. Interest below $10 is still taxable.

Is savings interest taxed as capital gains?

No. It is ordinary income, taxed at your marginal rate, which is usually higher than the long-term capital-gains rate.
