The APY formula

How APY is calculated, how to reverse it, and a worked example.

APY = (1 + r/n)n − 1, where r is the nominal annual interest rate as a decimal and n is the number of times interest compounds in a year — 365 for daily, 12 for monthly.

At 4.40% compounded daily: (1 + 0.044/365)365 − 1 = 0.04498, or 4.50% APY.

The formula

APY = (1 + r ÷ n)n − 1
  • r — the nominal annual rate, as a decimal (4.40% → 0.044)
  • n — compounding periods per year: 365 daily, 12 monthly, 4 quarterly, 1 annually

Worked example

A bank quotes 4.40%, compounded daily.

  1. Periodic rate: 0.044 ÷ 365 = 0.00012055
  2. Grow for a year: (1.00012055)365 = 1.04498
  3. Subtract one: 1.04498 − 1 = 0.04498
  4. APY: 4.50% (rounded)

Going the other way

From an APY back to the periodic rate, which is what a calculator actually needs:

periodic rate = (1 + APY)1/n − 1

This is the form this site's calculator uses. Starting from the APY the bank quotes, rather than from a nominal rate, is what guarantees that a 4.50% APY returns exactly 4.50% in a year — the property that defines APY in the first place. The full method.

Continuous compounding

As compounding becomes infinitely frequent, the formula approaches APY = er − 1. At 4.40% that is 4.4982% — two ten-thousandths of a point above daily compounding's 4.4980%. Beyond daily, frequency stops mattering in practice.

Questions

What is the APY formula?

APY = (1 + r/n)^n − 1, where r is the nominal annual rate as a decimal and n is the number of compounding periods per year.

How do I calculate APY from an interest rate?

Divide the rate by the compounding periods, add one, raise to the power of the periods, subtract one. At 4.40% daily that gives 4.50% APY.
