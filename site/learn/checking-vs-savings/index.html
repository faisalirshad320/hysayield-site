Checking vs savings: the main differences

One is for spending, one is for keeping. Here is what actually separates them.

A checking account is for spending: debit card, checks, bill pay and unlimited transactions, usually with little or no interest (the national average for interest checking is 0.07%).

A savings account is for money you are keeping: it pays interest (0.37% on average, far more at a high-yield account) but is less convenient to spend from. Most people need both.

What is a traditional savings account?

The standard savings account at a branch bank. It is convenient — same bank, same app, instant transfers — but pays around the national average of 0.37%: $37.00 a year on $10,000. A high-yield savings account is the same product with a higher rate, usually at an online bank, at the cost of one-to-three-day transfers.

How much to keep in each

  • Checking: a month or so of spending plus a buffer against overdrafts. Anything more earns next to nothing.
  • Savings: your emergency fund and near-term goals. The emergency fund calculator sizes it; how much to keep in a HYSA covers the rest.

Where a money market account fits

Between the two: savings-style interest with some checking features. How money market accounts work.

Questions

What are the main differences between a checking and savings account?

Checking is built for spending — debit card, checks, unlimited transactions, little interest. Savings is built for keeping — it pays interest but is less convenient to spend from, and some banks limit withdrawals.

Should I keep my money in checking or savings?

Keep what you will spend this month in checking and the rest in savings, where it earns interest. Both are insured to the same limits.

Can I have a checking and savings account at different banks?

Yes. Many people keep checking at a branch bank and savings at an online bank for the higher rate, and link the two for transfers.
