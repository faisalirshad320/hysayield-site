5-year CD rates

The national average 5-year CD rate, what it earns on common balances, and how it compares with other terms.

The national average rate on a 5-year CD is 1.38% (FDIC, September 21, 2026). At that rate $10,000 earns $709.31 over 5 years.

Competitive online banks usually pay well above the national average. At an example 4.50% APY the same $10,000 would earn $2,461.82 over 5 years — $1,752.51 more. Check the rate your bank actually quotes in the CD calculator.

5-year CD vs other terms

A 5-year CD currently averages 0.35 percentage points less than a 1-year CD (1.73%). Locking up money for longer is not being rewarded at the national-average level, so a longer term only makes sense if you find a rate well above the average or want to lock today's rate in case rates fall.

5-year CD vs a high-yield savings account

The national average savings rate is 0.37%, so $10,000 in an average savings account would earn about $186.37 over the same 5 years. A high-yield savings account usually pays far more than that, and you can withdraw at any time; a CD pays a fixed rate but charges a penalty for early withdrawal. If your HYSA rate is close to the CD rate, the flexibility usually wins. The CD vs HYSA calculator works out which pays more after a rate change, and the CD calculator shows the month an early-withdrawal penalty stops eating your interest.

Before you lock in

  • Early-withdrawal penalties are set by each bank, usually as some months of interest. Read the terms before you open.
  • FDIC or NCUA insurance covers CDs up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category.
  • Interest is taxable in the year it is credited, even if the CD has not matured — see how savings interest is taxed.
  • Laddering spreads money across several terms so part of it matures every year — try the CD ladder calculator.
