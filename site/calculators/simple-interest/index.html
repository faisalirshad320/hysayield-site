Simple interest calculator

I = P × r × t, worked out for you — with the compound figure alongside.

%

Simple interest = principal × rate × time (I = P × r × t). On $1,000 at 5% for 3 years that is 1,000 × 0.05 × 3 = $150. Interest is paid only on the original amount, never on earlier interest.

Savings accounts and CDs pay compound interest instead, which earns interest on interest. The calculator shows both, so you can see how much compounding adds.

The formula

I = P × r × t     A = P + I = P(1 + rt)
  • P — principal, the starting amount
  • r — annual rate as a decimal (5% → 0.05)
  • t — time in years (18 months → 1.5; 90 days → 90 ÷ 365)

Over one year they're identical. After that the gap widens every year — by year 30, compound interest earns more than double. That growing gap is why the rate on long-term savings matters so much, and why debt that compounds gets expensive.

Where simple interest is actually used

  • Many auto loans and some personal loans — interest accrues on the outstanding principal only.
  • Treasury bills and some short-term notes, quoted on a simple or discount basis.
  • Per-diem interest on a loan payoff or a late payment.
  • Not savings accounts or CDs, which compound — use the HYSA calculator or CD calculator for those.

Questions

How do you calculate simple interest?

Multiply the principal by the annual rate (as a decimal) by the time in years: I = P × r × t. $1,000 at 5% for 3 years is $150.

What is 4% interest on $10,000?

$400 a year in simple interest. Compounded yearly for several years it grows faster: $1,698.59 over 4 years versus $1,600 simple.

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is paid only on the original principal. Compound interest is also paid on interest already earned, so it grows faster the longer the money stays in.

How do you calculate simple interest for months or days?

Convert time to years first: divide months by 12 or days by 365, then apply I = P × r × t.
