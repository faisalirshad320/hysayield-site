How much does $100,000 earn in a high-yield savings account?

Interest on $100,000 at common APYs, from one month to ten years.

At 4.50% APY, $100,000 earns about $367.48 a month and $4,500.00 in the first year. Left alone for five years it grows to $124,618.

After a 22% federal and 5% state tax rate, the first year's interest drops to $3,285.00. The table below covers other rates and horizons; for your exact numbers use the calculator.

Reading the table

  • Across a row, interest grows slightly faster than linearly — that curve is compounding.
  • Down a column, doubling the APY roughly doubles the interest. The rate matters more than the compounding schedule by a wide margin.
  • The 0.50% row is roughly what a standard branch savings account pays. The gap to the rows below it is the case for a high-yield account.

$100,000 and the $1,000-a-month question

A common target is $1,000 a month in interest. At 4.50%, $100,000 produces $367.48 a month — about a third of the way. The balance that actually yields $1,000 a month at 4.50% is roughly $272,123, before tax; the reverse calculator works it out for any rate.

At this size a fifth of a percentage point is real money: 4.30% versus 4.50% is $200.00 a year. It is worth moving banks for, and worth checking that the headline APY applies to the whole balance rather than a tier.

What the table leaves out

  • Tax, charged as ordinary income each year — see how savings interest is taxed.
  • Rate changes. Every HYSA rate is variable; the five- and ten-year columns assume a flat rate that will not happen in practice.
  • Inflation, which is what separates a nominal return from a real one.
