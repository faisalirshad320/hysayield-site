How much does $25,000 earn in a high-yield savings account?

Interest on $25,000 at common APYs, from one month to ten years.

At 4.50% APY, $25,000 earns about $91.87 a month and $1,125.00 in the first year. Left alone for five years it grows to $31,155.

After a 22% federal and 5% state tax rate, the first year's interest drops to $821.25. The table below covers other rates and horizons; for your exact numbers use the calculator.

Reading the table

  • Across a row, interest grows slightly faster than linearly — that curve is compounding.
  • Down a column, doubling the APY roughly doubles the interest. The rate matters more than the compounding schedule by a wide margin.
  • The 0.50% row is roughly what a standard branch savings account pays. The gap to the rows below it is the case for a high-yield account.

$25,000: where tax starts to matter

At 4.50%, $25,000 earns $1,125.00 a year. In a 22% federal bracket with 5% state tax, $303.75 of that goes in tax, leaving $821.25. The bank reports the full amount on Form 1099-INT whether or not you withdraw it.

This is also the size at which many people hold more than they need in cash. If $25,000 is well above your emergency fund, the how much to keep in a HYSA guide covers what to do with the rest.

What the table leaves out

  • Tax, charged as ordinary income each year — see how savings interest is taxed.
  • Rate changes. Every HYSA rate is variable; the five- and ten-year columns assume a flat rate that will not happen in practice.
  • Inflation, which is what separates a nominal return from a real one.
